About

I am a PhD student in the Graduate School of Economics at Kyoto University, specializing in empirical industrial organization.

My research examines how competition shapes firms’ decisions about which products to offer, update, and discontinue. I study these questions in the automobile market, with a particular interest in how mergers affect product portfolios and consumer welfare.

Mergers and Product Repositioning
in the Automobile Market

How do mergers change the products firms choose to offer?

This ongoing project examines merger effects beyond prices by studying firms’ decisions to introduce new products, redesign existing models, and discontinue products. Using the Japanese automobile market, I aim to understand how changes in competition influence product portfolios and welfare.

Research approach

The project combines differentiated-product demand estimation with a model of firms’ product portfolio choices. A central question is how firms account for interactions among products in their own lineups when making entry, redesign, and exit decisions.

The research is in progress; a paper is not yet available on this website.
